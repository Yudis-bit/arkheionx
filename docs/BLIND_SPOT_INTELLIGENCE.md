# Blind Spot Intelligence (v5)

> Local/static heuristic review model. Blind spot candidates are **not**
> vulnerabilities. Criticality potential is **not** severity. Counterfactuals
> are research prompts, not findings. Human review is required for every
> conclusion.

ArkheionX v4 maps a protocol: where value enters, moves, and exits, the
assumptions that guard each path, and which paths have no tests. **ArkheionX v5
adds Blind Spot Intelligence: a layer that prioritizes where research attention
is weakest relative to how much could go wrong if a bug existed there.**

V4 helps answer "how does this protocol work?". V5 helps answer "what are we
probably missing?". It does not predict bugs. It identifies assumptions, unknown
surfaces, review-density gaps, and high-impact areas where expensive bugs are
*more likely to hide* — so a human or an AI agent spends limited review time
where it matters most.

This document defines the V5 vocabulary. The commands that produce it are
documented in [`V5_WORKFLOW.md`](V5_WORKFLOW.md) and
[`CLI_REFERENCE.md`](CLI_REFERENCE.md).

## Why this is a real capability, not a relabel

V4.1 already ranks coverage weakness into buckets. V5 goes further: it combines
**impact potential**, **review density**, **structural complexity**, and
**unproven assumptions** into a single transparent, explainable *blind spot
score*, maps **criticality potential** (blast radius) across every surface,
turns assumptions into **counterfactual research questions**, and packages the
whole thing into a research pack you can hand to a reviewer or an AI agent.

## Core terms

### 1. Surface

A protocol area worth review. ArkheionX derives surfaces from the review map and
research-surface engine. Surface kinds include: value entry, value exit,
accounting mutation, debt/credit/share/collateral mutation, liquidation, oracle
dependency, authorization, signature, Merkle proof, role/admin control,
periphery route, callback/external call, settlement, emergency path, and
upgrade/init path.

### 2. Review density (heuristic)

A heuristic estimate of how much local evidence appears to cover a surface.
Inputs include direct test references, function-name matches in tests, the
review map's test-gap status, and proof-plan coverage. Documentation and
comments are **not** counted as tests.

Labels: `strong`, `medium`, `weak`, `none`, `unknown`.

Review density is a heuristic. A `strong` label does not mean a surface is safe;
it means local test evidence was observed. Human review is still required.

### 3. Criticality potential (heuristic, not severity)

A heuristic estimate of **how bad it could be if a bug existed** in a surface —
its blast radius. Inputs include value exit, accounting/debt/collateral/share
mutation, authorization control, oracle dependency, liquidation/seizure,
settlement, callback/external call, periphery-to-core boundary, admin/emergency
power, and cross-contract dependency.

Labels: `very-high`, `high`, `medium`, `low`, `unknown`.

Criticality potential is **never** a severity. It does not claim a bug exists.
It estimates impact *conditional on* a bug existing, to help rank where a missed
bug would be most expensive. Do not submit criticality potential as a finding.

### 4. Blind spot candidate

A surface where criticality potential is high, review density is weak / none /
unknown, structural complexity or cross-module interaction is non-trivial, one
or more guarding assumptions are unproven, or a human/AI reviewer is likely to
skip it. A blind spot candidate is a **place to look**, not a bug.

The blind spot score combines four transparent components (see
[Scoring](#scoring-engine)). Every candidate lists the reasons that produced its
score.

### 5. Assumption

A condition that must hold for protocol safety or correctness. Examples: oracle
freshness, standard ERC20 behavior, authorization binding, non-reentrancy,
liquidation boundedness, share proportionality, monotonic reward index,
collateral availability, domain-separator correctness, Merkle-leaf completeness,
callback ordering, and the admin trust boundary.

Each assumption carries a source surface, why it matters, a counterfactual, a
local test direction, and the evidence that would settle it.

### 6. Counterfactual

A research question of the form **"what if this assumption is false?"**.
Counterfactuals are generated by negating important assumptions. Each is a
testable prompt with a local test direction, the evidence required, and a stop
condition. A counterfactual is not a finding and is never submitted on its own.

Example: *Assumption — a Merkle leaf binds every value-relevant field. What if
false — a proof for one claim authorizes a different claim shape or amount.
Local test — build two claims differing in one value-moving field and assert the
proof cannot authorize the second. Stop condition — if the leaf binds the full
field set and the test proves rejection, mark the hypothesis rejected.*

### 7. Unknown surface

A surface with insufficient local evidence to mark it reviewed, rejected, or
low-priority. Unknown does **not** mean vulnerable. Unknown means review
attention is not yet supported by evidence.

## Scoring engine

The blind spot score is transparent and additive. Nothing is hidden. Each
surface accumulates four component scores, and every contribution is reported
with a reason.

```text
blind_spot_score = impact_score + review_gap_score + complexity_score + assumption_score
```

- **impact_score** — value exit, accounting/debt/collateral/share mutation,
  authorization, liquidation/seizure, oracle dependency, external call/callback,
  periphery/core boundary, admin/emergency power, and cross-contract dependency
  each contribute points.
- **review_gap_score** — no direct test observed contributes most; unknown and
  partial coverage contribute less; a tested surface contributes nothing.
- **complexity_score** — external calls, loops, try/catch, Merkle/signature/
  domain logic, cross-contract calls, and periphery routes each contribute.
- **assumption_score** — unverified guarding assumptions contribute, with a
  bonus when several stack on one surface.

The total maps to a blind spot priority label:

| Score | Blind spot priority |
| ----- | ------------------- |
| 85+   | `very-high`         |
| 65–84 | `high`              |
| 40–64 | `medium`            |
| < 40  | `monitor`           |

The score is a **heuristic review priority**. It is never a probability, never a
severity, and never an exploitability estimate. The exact point values are
documented in `arkheionx/blind_spots/scoring.py` and can be tuned; the output
always explains the reasons behind a score.

## What the output always distinguishes

Every V5 artifact keeps these categories separate and labelled:

- **evidence** — a local test result or artifact a human recorded.
- **hypothesis** — a testable review prompt (status `open` until tested).
- **assumption** — a property a path appears to rely on.
- **blind-spot candidate** — a high-impact, weakly-reviewed surface to inspect.
- **criticality potential** — heuristic blast radius if a bug existed.
- **confirmed finding** — only ever from a human-provided evidence file.
- **rejected finding** — a hypothesis whose tested invariant held (useful memory).
- **human decision** — always made by a person; always the final field.

## Safety boundary

ArkheionX v5 is local and static. It requires no RPC, no live-chain calls, no
private keys, no secrets, and no deployed-contract scanning. It does not automate
exploitation, does not broadcast transactions, does not auto-submit reports, and
does not assign severity. It does not predict vulnerabilities and is not a
replacement for an audit.

Blind spot detection is a heuristic and is not perfect. It can miss surfaces and
it can rank a well-reviewed surface highly. Use it to allocate attention, then
let a human make every security call.


## What comes next: V6 evidence classification

Blind Spot Intelligence (v5) answers *where should I look first?*. The v6
[Evidence Graph](EVIDENCE_GRAPH.md) and [Interaction Matrix](INTERACTION_MATRIX.md)
answer the next question: *what evidence exists, what remains unresolved, and
which interactions still lack proof?*

> V5 shows where to look. V6 shows what is proven, what is unresolved, and which
> interactions still lack evidence.

V6 takes the same surfaces and classifies each into an evidence state (tested,
rejected-with-evidence, confirmed-candidate, unresolved, insufficient-evidence,
needs-human-review, unclassified, out-of-scope), detects meaningful interactions
between surfaces, and maps everything still unresolved. See
[`V6_WORKFLOW.md`](V6_WORKFLOW.md). The same boundaries hold: an evidence state is
not a vulnerability claim, interaction priority is not severity, and human review
is required.
